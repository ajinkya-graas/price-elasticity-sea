ℹ️  What is this? How does it work?

▼

This tool measures price elasticity of demand across Puma Factory Outlet Centre (FOC) physical retail stores in SEA — how much quantity sold changes when a product's price changes. Unlike the marketplace version, FOC data comes from billing records rather than online orders, and does not have campaign sale days (D-days).

Step 1
Collect billing data
Billing-line data (Style No., store, net price, qty) from Puma FOC stores across 6 SEA markets. Division and RBU come directly from the billing system.
Step 2
Clean & filter
Returns (negative qty) and near-free promotional sales (price < 5% of RRP) are excluded. Per-unit price = Net invoice ÷ Billed qty.
Step 3
Log-log regression
For each article (style) we fit an OLS regression on log(price) vs. log(quantity), aggregated to daily (store × date) level. The slope is the elasticity coefficient. Requires ≥5 distinct price points.
Step 4
Filter & compare
Slice by store, division, RBU, article, and date range. All charts recalculate live — no server needed.

How to read the elasticity number

Below −1 · Elastic
Demand drops more than proportionally when price rises. Customers are price-sensitive — discounting drives significant volume uplift.
−1 to 0 · Inelastic
Demand holds up even as price changes. Brand loyalty or lack of alternatives. You have pricing power here.
Positive · Anomalous
Higher price correlates with more demand — possible for luxury/status goods, or a data artefact. Interpret with caution.
Data coverage: Top 1000 articles per market by volume · Sales at price < 5% of RRP and returns excluded · Price elasticity in FOC context reflects markdown behaviour across stores and dates.
